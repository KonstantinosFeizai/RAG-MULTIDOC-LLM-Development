# International Racquet Sports Federation — Official Rules Handbook (2026 Edition)

## 1. Sport Overview & Core Principles

The International Racquet Sports Federation (IRSF) governs competitive and recreational play for both **Tennis** and **Padel**. While both sports share roots in racquet-and-ball scoring, they differ fundamentally in court architecture, equipment, and wall-play mechanics.

Our three core officiating principles are:

1. **Continuous Play:** Players must maintain a steady tempo; maximum **25 seconds** are permitted between points in both sports.
2. **Code of Conduct:** Verbal abuse, racquet throwing, or ball abuse results in progressive penalties (Warning -> Point Penalty -> Game Penalty -> Default).
3. **Hindrance Rule:** Any deliberate distraction (grunting loudly at impact, waving arms, or shouting during an opponent's shot) results in an immediate **loss of point**.

---

## 2. Court Dimensions & Equipment Specifications

- **Tennis Court:** Measures **23.77 m** long by **8.23 m** wide (singles) or **10.97 m** wide (doubles). Net height is **0.914 m** at the center and **1.07 m** at the posts. Out-of-bounds lines are strictly terminal—the ball is dead the moment it bounces outside the painted lines.
- **Padel Court:** Measures **20 m** long by **10 m** wide, completely enclosed by tempered glass walls (**3 m to 4 m** high) and metallic wire mesh (**3 m** high). Net height is **0.88 m** at the center and **0.92 m** at the posts. Played exclusively in **doubles**.
- **Racquets & Balls:**
  - _Tennis:_ Strung racquets (max **73.7 cm** length) and pressurized felt balls (6.54–6.86 cm diameter).
  - _Padel:_ Solid, perforated composite paddles without strings (max **45.5 cm** length) with a mandatory **wrist safety cord**. Balls have slightly lower internal pressure than tennis balls for controlled wall rebounds.

## 3. Scoring System & Match Format

Both sports use identical traditional scoring hierarchies, with one key modification at deuce in modern tournament play:

- **Point Progression:** **0 (Love) -> 15 -> 30 -> 40 -> Game**.
- **Deuce & Golden Point:**
  - _Tennis:_ Traditional **Advantage** scoring requires winning by 2 consecutive points at 40–40 (Deuce), except in specific doubles circuits that use No-Ad scoring.
  - _Padel:_ Official FIP/Premier Padel tournaments enforce the **Golden Point (Punto de Oro)** at 40–40—the receiving team chooses which side receives the serve, and the winner of that single point wins the game.
- **Sets & Tiebreaks:** A set is won by the first side to reach **6 games** with a 2-game margin. At **6–6**, a 7-point tiebreak (win by 2) is played. Matches are typically **Best-of-3 sets** (Grand Slam Men's Singles in Tennis uses Best-of-5).

## 4. Service Rules & Faults

- **Tennis Serve:**
  - Hit **overhead** (or underhand) from behind the baseline before the ball bounces.
  - Must land diagonally in the opponent's service box without touching the net (a net-cord touch that lands in is a **Let** and replayed).
  - Players get **two serves** per point; missing both results in a **Double Fault** (loss of point).
- **Padel Serve:**
  - Must be hit **underhand** at or below **waist height** after bouncing the ball once behind the service line.
  - Must land diagonally in the opponent's service box. After bouncing in the box, the ball **may strike the glass wall** (valid), **cannot strike the metallic wire mesh** (fault), and **cannot bounce twice** before being returned.
  - Players get **two serves** per point. A ball hitting the net and landing in the box before hitting the glass is a **Let**; hitting the wire mesh after a net-cord touch is a **Fault**.

---

## 5. Rally Rules & Wall Play

- **Tennis Rally Mechanics:**
  - The ball may bounce **at most once** on the receiver's side before being hit back over the net.
  - Volleying (hitting the ball before it bounces) is permitted anywhere on the court **except on the return of serve**.
  - Players may never touch the net, reach over the net to strike a ball before it crosses, or allow the ball to touch their body or clothing.
- **Padel Rally Mechanics (Wall & Mesh Rules):**
  - After bouncing once on the ground, the ball may rebound off **any glass wall or metallic wire mesh** on the receiver's side before being returned over the net.
  - **Attacking Off Your Own Glass:** Players may intentionally hit the ball against their own tempered glass wall to send it over the net to the opponent's court. Hitting the ball directly into your own wire mesh or the opponent's walls without bouncing on the court first is an immediate **loss of point**.
  - **Out-of-Court Play ("Punto por Fuera"):** If a smash bounces in the opponent's court and rebounds out over the 3 m or 4 m walls, a player is permitted to run outside the court doors and hit the ball back into play before its second bounce (provided external clearance zones exist).

---

## 6. Changeovers & Coaching Guidelines

- **Changeovers:** Players switch ends of the court after **odd-numbered games** (1, 3, 5, etc.) in each set. Break times are **90 seconds** on odd-game changeovers (except after Game 1, which is continuous with no rest) and **120 seconds** between sets.
- **On-Court Coaching:**
  - _Tennis:_ Off-court coaching (concise verbal cues when the player is at the same end, or hand signals) is permitted during changeovers and between points, provided it does not interrupt play.
  - _Padel:_ Coaches are seated courtside and may provide tactical instructions during the **90-second changeovers** and **120-second set breaks** only.

---

## 7. Frequently Asked Questions (Official Rulings)

- **What happens if a ball hits the top of the net and drops directly over during a rally?** In both Tennis and Padel, the point continues live—net-cord touches during a rally are legal unless the ball lands out of bounds (or hits the wire mesh directly on a Padel serve).
- **Can a Padel player hit a volley directly after a serve?** No. Just like in Tennis, the return of serve in Padel **must bounce once** on the court before the receiving player strikes it.
- **Who wins the point if a Padel smash hits the court, bounces over the 4 m back wall, and cannot be retrieved?** The smashing team wins the point, as the ball bounced legally inside the opponent's court and was not returned before bouncing a second time outside the enclosure.
