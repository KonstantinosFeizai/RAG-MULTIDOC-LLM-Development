# International Association Football Federation — Official Laws of the Game Handbook (2026 Edition)

## 1. Sport Overview & Core Principles

The International Association Football Federation (IAFF) governs competitive and recreational **Association Football (Soccer)** worldwide. Matches are contested between two teams of 11 players each, with the objective of scoring more goals than the opponent within regulation time.

Our three core officiating principles are:

1. **Spirit of the Game:** Laws are enforced to protect player safety, ensure fairness, and maintain flowing, uninterrupted play whenever possible.
2. **Advantage Rule:** The referee allows play to continue when the team against which an offense has been committed stands to benefit from such an advantage, penalizing the original offense only if the anticipated advantage does not ensue within a few seconds.
3. **Sole Authority:** The decisions of the referee regarding facts connected with play—including whether or not a goal is scored and the result of the match—are final.

---

## 2. Field of Play, Ball & Equipment Specifications

- **Field Dimensions:** Rectangular natural or approved artificial turf measuring **100 m to 110 m** in length (touchlines) and **64 m to 75 m** in width (goal lines) for international matches.
  - _Penalty Area:_ Extends **16.5 m** from the inside of each goalpost and **16.5 m** into the field of play. The penalty mark sits **11 m** directly in front of the center of the goal.
  - _Goal Dimensions:_ **7.32 m** wide between the inside of the posts and **2.44 m** high from the ground to the lower edge of the crossbar.
- **The Ball:** Spherical, made of suitable material, with a circumference of **68 cm to 70 cm**, weight between **410 g and 450 g**, and internal pressure of **0.6 to 1.1 atmospheres** at sea level.
- **Players & Equipment:**
  - **11 players per team** on the field (including one designated goalkeeper); a match cannot start or continue if either team has fewer than **7 players**.
  - Mandatory gear consists of a jersey with sleeves, shorts, socks, shinguards completely covered by socks, and footwear. Jewelry is strictly prohibited.
  - Up to **5 substitutions** are permitted per team in standard competitive matches across a maximum of **3 substitution windows** (excluding halftime).

---

## 3. Match Duration, Scoring & Tiebreakers

- **Regulation Time:** Two equal halves of **45 minutes** each, separated by a halftime interval not exceeding **15 minutes**.
- **Allowance for Time Lost (Stoppage Time):** The referee adds time at the end of each half for substitutions, injury assessments, time-wasting, disciplinary sanctions, and VAR checks.
- **Method of Scoring:** A goal is scored when the **whole of the ball** passes completely over the goal line, between the goalposts and under the crossbar, provided no offense was committed by the scoring team.
- **Knockout Tiebreakers:** When competition rules require a winning team after a drawn match:
  - _Extra Time:_ Two equal periods of **15 minutes** each, with no halftime break (only a brief interval to switch ends).
  - _Penalty Shootout (Kicks from the Penalty Mark):_ Teams alternate **5 kicks** each; if still tied, sudden-death single rounds continue until one team scores and the other misses from an equal number of kicks.

## 4. Offside Rule & Restarts

- **Offside Position:** A player is in an offside position if any part of their **head, body, or feet** is in the opponents' half (excluding the halfway line) AND nearer to the opponents' goal line than both the ball and the **second-last opponent** (arms and hands are excluded for all players).
- **Offside Offense:** Being in an offside position is not an offense in itself. It is penalized only if, at the moment the ball is played or touched by a teammate, the player becomes involved in active play by:
  - Interfering with play (playing or touching the ball).
  - Interfering with an opponent (preventing an opponent from playing the ball or obstructing their line of vision).
  - Gaining an advantage by playing the ball after it rebounds off a goalpost, crossbar, match official, or opponent.
  - _Exceptions:_ No offside offense exists if a player receives the ball directly from a **goal kick**, **throw-in**, or **corner kick**.
- **Restarts of Play:**
  - _Kick-off:_ Starts both halves and follows every goal; the ball is in play once kicked and clearly moves (may be kicked in any direction).
  - _Throw-in:_ Awarded when the whole ball crosses the touchline; the thrower must face the field, keep part of both feet on or behind the touchline, and throw with both hands from behind and over the head.
  - _Goal Kick & Corner Kick:_ Awarded when the whole ball crosses the goal line without a goal being scored—a goal kick if last touched by an attacker, a corner kick if last touched by a defender.

## 5. Fouls, Handball & Disciplinary Sanctions

- **Direct Free Kick Offenses:** Awarded if a player carelessly, recklessly, or using excessive force commits offenses including kicking, tripping, charging, jumping at, pushing, striking, or holding an opponent. If committed by a defender inside their own penalty area, a **Penalty Kick** is awarded.
- **Handball Rule:** It is an offense if a player:
  - Deliberately touches the ball with their hand/arm (moving the hand/arm toward the ball).
  - Touches the ball with their hand/arm when it has made their body **unnaturally bigger** (not justified by natural body movement for that specific situation).
  - Scores in the opponents' goal directly from their hand/arm (even if accidental) or immediately after the ball touches their hand/arm.
- **Indirect Free Kick Offenses:** Awarded for playing in a dangerous manner, impeding the progress of an opponent without contact, preventing the goalkeeper from releasing the ball, or goalkeeper handling infractions (e.g., holding the ball for more than **8 seconds**, or handling a deliberate pass-back kicked by a teammate).
- **Disciplinary Cards:**
  - _Yellow Card (Caution):_ Issued for unsporting behavior, dissent, persistent infringement, delaying the restart of play, failing to respect required distance (**9.15 m** on free kicks/corners), or entering/leaving the field without permission.
  - _Red Card (Sending-Off):_ Issued for serious foul play, violent conduct, spitting/biting, denying an obvious goal-scoring opportunity (**DOGSO**) via handball or non-ball-playing foul, offensive/abusive language, or receiving a **second yellow card** in the same match. Sent-off players cannot be replaced.

## 6. Video Assistant Referee (VAR) Protocol

- **Scope of Review:** VAR intervenes **only** for "clear and obvious errors" or "serious missed incidents" in four match-changing categories:
  1. **Goals / No Goals** (fouls, offside, ball out of play in the attacking build-up).
  2. **Penalty / No Penalty** decisions.
  3. **Direct Red Card** incidents (excluding second yellow cards).
  4. **Mistaken Identity** when awarding a yellow or red card.
- **Review Process:** Factual decisions (such as offside lines or whether the ball crossed a boundary line) are confirmed directly by the VAR room. Subjective decisions (fouls, handballs, red cards) require the on-field referee to conduct an **On-Field Review (OFR)** at the pitchside monitor before overturning the original call.

## 7. Frequently Asked Questions (Official Rulings)

- **Can a player score directly from a throw-in or an indirect free kick?** No. If the ball enters either goal directly from a throw-in or an indirect free kick without touching another player, a **goal kick** (if into the opponents' goal) or a **corner kick** (if into the kicker's own goal) is awarded.
- **What happens if the ball hits the referee during play?** If the ball touches a match official and remains on the field, play is stopped and restarted with a **dropped ball** if a team starts a promising attack, the ball goes directly into the goal, or ball possession changes teams. Otherwise, play continues.
- **When is a penalty kick retaken?** A penalty kick is retaken if the goalkeeper moves off the goal line with both feet before the ball is kicked and saves the shot, or if a defender encroaches into the penalty area and affects the outcome after a missed kick. If an attacker encroaches and the goal is scored, the kick is also retaken.
